ENCOUNTER 1 — ROBERT KLEIN, 8:00 AM
Visit Type: Annual Wellness Visit (routine preventive exam; no acute concerns reported)
Capture Settings:
  - Note template: Comprehensive preventive/wellness template with Health Maintenance (screenings, immunizations) and Preventive Counseling sections enabled.
  - ROS / exam scope: Full multi-system ROS and complete multi-system exam.
  - Detail level: Detailed, with an extended capture window for the longest visit of the session.
  - Pre-visit context: Problem list, medication list, and screening/immunization history linked before the visit.

ENCOUNTER 2 — MARIA CHEN, 8:40 AM
Visit Type: Acute Care Visit (sore throat and fever for three days; rule out streptococcal pharyngitis)
Capture Settings:
  - Note template: Problem-focused acute (SOAP) template for a single problem.
  - ROS / exam scope: Focused: constitutional, ENT, respiratory, skin, lymphatic; pertinent positives and negatives captured explicitly.
  - Detail level: Concise.
  - HPI priorities: Onset and duration, maximum temperature, aggravating factors, treatments tried, sick contacts/exposures.
  - Section routing: Point-of-care test orders, conditional (if positive / if negative) treatment branches, and patient counseling to Plan.
Reasoning: An acute visit is short and narrow, and the diagnosis turns on a few pertinent findings (fever, tonsillar exudate, tender cervical nodes, absence of cough), so each must be captured with the correct positive/negative status. Exposure history raises the pre-test probability of strep and must be prompted for in the HPI. The same-day rapid test creates a branching plan that must be recorded exactly, and counseling given at the end of a brief visit is easily misrouted.
Verification focus carried into Component 2: positive/negative status of findings, exposure history, plan branches and counseling placement.

Capture Settings:
  - Note template: Chronic-care follow-up template with problem-based Assessment and Plan (one entry per problem).
  - Pre-visit context: Prior plan, most recent HbA1c and metabolic panel, and current medication list linked before the visit.
  - HPI priorities: Home glucose and blood pressure readings recorded verbatim, medication adherence, new interval symptoms.

With several problems discussed, problem-based Assessment and Plan with strict routing keeps orders and referrals out of the Assessment.
Verification focus carried into Component 2: verbatim home values, new interval symptoms, discrete foot-exam findings, routing of orders and referrals to Plan.

COMPONENT 2: SECTION-LEVEL VERIFICATION REVIEWS
========================================================================

Scope and method: Encounters 2 and 3 (Encounter 1 was signed earlier and is excluded). Each draft section was compared, in note order, with the matching source items, then every source item was traced to a note section to detect omissions. The Component 1 focus checks were applied to each note.

Classification rule: Omission = source item absent from the note. Inaccuracy = a source item recorded with a changed value or status. Addition = a finding or statement the encounter does not support, including invented findings with their own descriptors where the source recorded none. Structural deviation = content placed in the wrong section. Severity (High/Moderate/Low) reflects potential clinical impact. “Linked” entries are downstream effects of another entry and are counted once in Component 3.

COMPONENT 3: QUALITY EVALUATION SUMMARY
========================================================================

Evidence base: 17 entries across 12 reviewed sections; 13 distinct discrepancies once linked entries are counted once. Sections verified without discrepancy: 3 of 12 (Encounter 2 Chief Complaint and Assessment; Encounter 3 Chief Complaint). Distinct discrepancies by type: Addition 5, Inaccuracy 3, Structural Deviation 3, Omission 2. Five are rated High severity.

CLINICAL ACCURACY
Rating: Not Met
Supporting Observation: Eight distinct discrepancies (3 inaccuracies, 5 additions) change the clinical record, and four High-severity discrepancies turn a documented negative or normal finding into a positive or abnormal one: cough (E2-3), truncal rash (E2-5), exertional chest tightness (E3-1/E3-3), and diminished pedal pulses (E3-6). If signed, these would remove a strep diagnostic criterion, suggest scarlet fever or confuse assessment of any later amoxicillin rash, document an unaddressed anginal-type symptom in a diabetic patient, and imply peripheral arterial disease.

COMPLETENESS
Rating: Partially Met
Supporting Observation: Most source content was captured correctly, including all vital signs, home readings, medication doses, and the conditional antibiotic plan. However, each note omitted its most decision-relevant history item, the household strep exposure (E2-1) and the new toe numbness that supports the neuropathy diagnosis (E3-2), and each Plan is missing a discussed item (E2-7, E3-10).

STRUCTURAL CONSISTENCY
Rating: Partially Met
Supporting Observation: Both notes use the expected six-section structure in the correct order for their visit types. However, both placed plan content outside the Plan, with counseling in the HPI (E2-2) and the podiatry referral in the Assessment (E3-7), and the Encounter 2 ROS includes an exam-only finding (E2-4).

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION 1 (PRIMARY)
Pattern Identified: Reversal of documented negative or normal findings. Four distinct discrepancies (five entries) convert a negative or normal finding into a positive or abnormal one: E2-3, E2-5, E3-1/E3-3, E3-6. They occur in both notes and in three section types (HPI, ROS, Physical Examination), all are rated High severity, and two (E2-3, E2-5) make the draft contradict itself.
Why It Matters: A false positive finding is harder to catch than a gap because it reads as plausible clinical content, and once signed it becomes part of the legal medical record. These entries alter diagnostic criteria, document symptoms that have no matching assessment or plan (a compliance and liability gap), and could appear to support diagnoses or services the encounter did not justify.
Proposed Corrective Action:
  1. Add a mandatory pre-signature step: the provider checks every positive symptom and abnormal exam finding against the encounter evidence, starting with the items flagged in Component 1 (cough and rash for acute visits; chest pain, numbness, and pedal pulses for chronic-care follow-ups).
  2. Add a contradiction screen to the quality team’s review that flags any finding recorded as both present and absent in the same note (this would have caught E2-3 and E2-5).
  3. Audit all Dragon Copilot notes for these reversals for 30 days, report the rate to the clinic director, and send examples to the configuration owner; then move to a 10% weekly sample once four consecutive weeks show no reversals.

IMPROVEMENT RECOMMENDATION 2
Pattern Identified: Plan items placed outside the Plan section in both notes: counseling in the HPI (E2-2/E2-7) and the podiatry referral in the Assessment (E3-7/E3-10).
Why It Matters: Staff act on orders and referrals from the Plan. A referral listed only in the Assessment may never be placed, and counseling missing from the Plan may not reach the patient’s visit instructions.
Proposed Corrective Action: Enforce the Component 1 routing setting (orders, referrals, and counseling go to Plan only) on all templates, and add a review step that matches each item in the source’s “Assessment and Plan Discussed” list to a line in the Plan before signing.
